Industries / Logistics and transportation

Every load gets typed in three times before it is paid.

A carrier or a small 3PL runs on a dispatch board, an ELD platform and an accounting file, and the same load is keyed into each one by hand. We connect those systems, put a voice agent on the check-call phone, and turn detention, empty kilometres and unbilled accessorials into numbers that assemble themselves. A dispatcher still decides who drives what.

How it actually runs

Before anything is built, this is the week we are describing.

No software can be scoped from a category name. These are the specifics of logistics and transportation that decide what is worth building and what is not.

  1. 01

    The rate confirmation arrives as a PDF and gets re-typed

    A broker emails a rate con. Dispatch reads it, keys the shipper, consignee, pickup window, rate and reference numbers into the TMS, and then the driver gets the same details by text. At delivery the BOL is photographed, emailed back, and someone matches it to the load again so the invoice can go out. One load, three or four manual entries, and the reference number is wrong on one of them.

  2. 02

    The phone rings all day with the same three questions

    Where is my truck. Can you cover a load tomorrow from Surrey to Calgary. What is your rate to Edmonton. The dispatcher who answers is the same person building tomorrow's board, so every check call costs a planning minute. The GPS answer is on the ELD screen already. The rate answer is in last month's invoices. Neither gets to the caller without a person looking it up.

  3. 03

    Detention happens, and most of it never gets billed

    A driver sits at a dock for three hours. The ELD knows exactly when the truck arrived and left, because it was geofenced and logging the whole time. But the detention claim depends on the driver remembering to note it, dispatch remembering to add the accessorial, and the office chasing the broker for it. Fleets charge for detention on paper and collect on a fraction of it.

  4. 04

    The weekly numbers are built in a spreadsheet on Sunday

    Revenue per truck, cost per kilometre, empty kilometres, fuel per litre, driver pay as a share of revenue. Owners want these every week and get them once a month, if the bookkeeper is caught up. The data exists in the TMS, the ELD platform, the fuel card statement and the accounting file, and it takes one person a Sunday afternoon to line it up.

  5. 05

    Compliance lives in expiry dates nobody is watching

    Driver licences, medicals, insurance certificates, CVIP inspections, IFTA filings, the National Safety Code profile and the annual carrier renewal all expire on their own schedule. Small fleets track them in a binder or a shared spreadsheet. The first warning is usually a roadside inspection, a rejected load from a shipper's compliance check or an insurer asking for a document nobody can find.

What we build

6 builds that pay for themselves here.

Each one states what it reads, what it does, and what a person still approves. Scope is agreed after the audit, one build at a time, against a baseline you sign.

Voice agent for check calls and quotes

Answers the dispatch line. For a check call it reads the load reference, pulls the last ELD position and the current ETA, and tells the caller. For a quote it reads the lane and your rate history, gives a range you set, and books a callback. Anything about a claim, a damaged load or an angry shipper transfers to a person with the transcript attached. A dispatcher confirms every quote before it becomes a rate.

Measured by: Calls resolved without a dispatcher picking up, per week

Rate confirmation and BOL parsing

Reads incoming rate cons, BOLs and PODs from the dispatch inbox and the driver app. Extracts shipper, consignee, windows, rate, references and weights, and creates a draft order in the TMS with a confidence score on each field. Low-confidence fields are highlighted. Dispatch reviews the draft and promotes it in one click. At delivery the POD is matched to the load so invoicing can start the same day.

Measured by: Minutes from rate con received to order live in the TMS

Detention and accessorial recovery

Reads geofence arrival and departure stamps from the ELD platform for every stop, compares them to the appointment window and the free time in the customer's contract, and flags every stop where detention is owed. Drafts the accessorial line with the time stamps as evidence and attaches it to the invoice. The office approves each claim before it goes out; nothing is billed automatically.

Measured by: Detention dollars invoiced per month

Driver and carrier onboarding with expiry tracking

New drivers and partner carriers upload licence, abstract, medical, insurance certificate and safety fitness certificate through a form. The system reads each document, checks the expiry date and the name against the application, and builds the file. It then watches every expiry date across the fleet and warns 60, 30 and 7 days out. The safety manager approves each driver before they are dispatchable.

Measured by: Expired documents found at roadside or by a shipper audit

Dispatch board assistant

Reads tomorrow's open loads, every driver's remaining hours of service, current truck positions and home time commitments. Proposes a plan that covers the loads with the least empty distance and no hours violations, and shows the empty kilometres each option costs. The dispatcher drags, changes or rejects any line. No driver is assigned and no load is accepted or declined without a dispatcher approving it.

Measured by: Empty kilometres as a share of total kilometres

Weekly fleet report that cites its sources

Every Monday the system assembles revenue per truck, revenue per loaded kilometre, cost per kilometre, empty share, fuel litres per 100 kilometres by truck, detention billed and collected, and receivables past 30 days. Every number carries a link to the TMS, ELD or accounting records it came from, so a doubtful figure can be traced in seconds. The owner reads it. Nobody builds it.

Measured by: Hours the office spends assembling numbers each week

Where the data comes from

Your systems of record stay exactly where they are.

We read them, we do not replace them. Each one below says what we connect to, how, and the line the build does not cross.

Dispatch and TMS

Usually: Axon Software (Saskatoon), Tailwind TMS (Vancouver), Rose Rocket (Toronto), Trimble TruckMate, McLeod LoadMaster

What it holds. Orders, loads, stops, drivers and equipment assigned, rates, accessorials, customer invoices and driver settlements.

How we connect. API where the vendor offers one (Rose Rocket, Tailwind and TruckMate do), otherwise a scheduled export or a read-only database replica. We write new orders in as draft records with the source document attached.

Where it stops. We never assign a driver, change a rate or issue an invoice without a dispatcher or the office approving it. Drafts wait in a queue; a person promotes them.

ELD and telematics

Usually: Geotab (Oakville), ISAAC Instruments (Quebec), Samsara, Motive, Trimble

What it holds. Hours of service logs, GPS breadcrumbs, geofence arrivals and departures, engine faults, fuel consumption and driver behaviour events.

How we connect. Read-only API pull on a schedule, plus webhooks where the platform fires them for arrivals and departures. Location and time stamps flow into the load record and the detention calculation.

Where it stops. Hours of service logs are never edited, annotated or certified by our software. Only the driver and the carrier's safety person touch a record of duty status.

Accounting and fuel

Usually: QuickBooks Online, Sage 50 Canadian, Xero, fuel card statements from Petro-Canada SuperPass, Shell Fleet, Comdata

What it holds. Customer invoices, receivables, payables, driver pay, fuel purchases by card and province, and the IFTA inputs.

How we connect. QuickBooks and Xero by API. Sage 50 by scheduled export. Fuel card statements by parsing the monthly CSV or PDF, with each line matched to a truck and a trip.

Where it stops. We post invoices as drafts and match payments as suggestions. A bookkeeper approves every posting. Nothing is filed with CRA or a provincial fuel tax authority by the system.

Built around your rules

The regimes that govern this work, and how the build answers each one.

Constraints come first, because they decide the architecture. Bring us your hosting, residency and regulatory rules at the start and we design to them rather than around them.

Regulatory and professional obligations that shape a build in logistics and transportation.
Regime What it demands here How the build complies
Commercial Vehicle Drivers Hours of Service Regulations (Transport Canada) and the ELD mandate Federally regulated carriers must record hours of service on an ELD certified by an accredited third party. Records of duty status belong to the driver and the carrier, must be retained, and edits must be annotated and approved by the driver. Our systems read hours of service and location data from the certified ELD platform and never write to it. No log is edited, certified or annotated by software we build. Remaining hours are used only to propose dispatch plans that a person approves.
National Safety Code, provincial carrier safety programmes (BC NSC safety certificate, Ontario CVOR) Carriers must hold a safety fitness certificate, keep driver files, inspection records and hours of service records for set periods, and their profile is scored on inspections, collisions and convictions. Driver and vehicle files are built from parsed documents with retention dates set to the regulated period. Expiry warnings run automatically. The safety manager approves every driver file before dispatch and nothing is deleted before its retention date.
PIPEDA and BC PIPA GPS positions, hours of service and driver behaviour events are personal information about an identifiable employee. Collection must be for a reasonable purpose, drivers must be told, and access must be limited and logged. Location and behaviour data are used for dispatch, safety and billing only, with role-based access and an audit log of every read. Drivers are told what is collected and why. Where a carrier requires it, every model and every byte is hosted in Canada, and we design to that constraint before the first line of code.
Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) Commercial email or text to shippers, brokers and prospects needs express or implied consent, sender identification and a working unsubscribe. Capacity emails and lane offers go only to contacts with recorded consent or an existing business relationship within the CASL window. Every message carries the carrier's identity and an unsubscribe that is honoured within ten business days. Transactional messages about a live load are kept separate from marketing sends.

What we will not automate

  • We will not edit, annotate or certify a driver's hours of service log. Only the driver and the carrier's safety person touch a record of duty status.
  • We will not accept a load, assign a driver or file a CBSA eManifest without a dispatcher approving it. The system drafts; a person commits.

A person stays in the loop

Anything that spends money, sends something irreversible, or carries a professional obligation arrives as a draft with a named reviewer. The system prepares the work. A person decides whether it ships.

You own the code and the data at the end of the engagement.
