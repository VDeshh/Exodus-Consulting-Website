Why this page exists

A proposal is not a project. A build in progress is not a launch.

Most consulting portfolios flatten everything into one tense, so a quote from last spring reads the same as a system running tonight. Ours does not. The three engagements below are the ones with nothing shipped behind them yet, and putting them on their own page is the easiest way to keep the other case studies honest.

Matchbox Global is under construction: real work, no launch metric yet. Market Meats and Royal Feet stopped at the proposal. The scoping was real work with a real number attached, so we list it, and we say plainly that nothing was built and no result is claimed.

Engagement 01

Matchbox Global In build

Matchbox Global is a UK matchmaking platform. We are building the technology layer: taking complicated matching logic and turning it into software that stays clean as it scales, and moving the product from architecture toward production.

Matching is the whole product, so the logic is the whole risk. Rules that read fine on a whiteboard turn into branching special cases the moment real people and real preferences arrive, and that is what gets rewritten at scale. Our job is to make that layer explicit and testable before the platform depends on it.

It is in build. There is no launch metric to quote, no screenshot to show and no result to claim yet. When there is one, it will appear with its arithmetic next to it, the same as everything else on this site.

Engagement 02

Market Meats, Kerrisdale Proposed

An independent butcher at 2233 W 41st Ave in Vancouver, owner Zul Suleman. A single counter with a loyal local trade and no way to sell to it once the shop was shut.

We scoped two things together: an SEO growth plan for a single-location butcher competing on local search, and an e-commerce and continuity plan so the business kept earning when the door was closed. The build was a "Market Meats at Home" online ordering site covering about 200 SKUs, with custom buy links across the catalogue and custom links for the top five best sellers, plus Square POS integration so the counter and the site read the same catalogue.

Quoted at $3,000 for the website plus $500 for the Square POS integration. It was never built. There is no traffic figure, no order volume and no result to report, because nothing went live.

Engagement 03

Royal Feet Reflexology Proposed

A local business whose reputation was real but hard to find. Reviews sat around 4.2 stars across roughly 135 reviews, scattered across platforms rather than consolidated in one place a searcher would land on.

That is a findability problem before it is a rating problem. A searcher comparing two nearby options does not add up five partial profiles, they read the first one that looks complete. So we scoped a local SEO growth plan around consolidating the review profile and the local listing: one profile worth finding instead of five partial ones.

It was never built. The 4.2 stars and the roughly 135 reviews are the starting point we measured, not an outcome we produced, and no ranking or review movement is claimed.
