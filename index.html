Where things actually stand

Almost everyone is using AI. Almost nobody can point at the money.

Three numbers from three separate 2024 and 2025 studies, each one printed with its source.

88%

of companies report using AI. About 6% attribute more than 5% of profit to it.

McKinsey, State of AI, 2025
95%

of enterprise AI pilots show no measurable P&L return.

MIT NANDA, The GenAI Divide, 2025
80%+

of AI projects fail, about double the failure rate of other IT projects.

RAND, 2024

The tools are not the problem. Projects fail on everything around the tool: no agreed baseline, no owner in the room, and no training for the people expected to use it. Deloitte found companies spend about 93% of AI budgets on technology and about 7% on the people (Deloitte, 2025). We work it the other way round.

Owners do not call us about AI. They call us about three sentences.

What we build

Three kinds of work, and the third one waits for the first two.

Every engagement starts with the audit. Nothing gets built before the arithmetic is on the page.

01 AI opportunity audit

We map every function and score the opportunities on return and feasibility. Uniserve got a mapped view of where support time was going, then a shortlist of the places AI would actually pay, with the arithmetic attached.

02 Custom software

When the process is the problem, we write the software. Palapa Tours got tip-out software that calculates, splits and tracks staff gratuities. B-Side Group's inventory software is in build. Fennec is the full version of that idea.

03 Agent networks and copilots

Once the data is clean and the process is real, agents are worth building. Ferry AI coordinates agents across ops, guests, campaigns, inventory and service inside Fennec. The Uniserve chatbot and voice agent are in build.
