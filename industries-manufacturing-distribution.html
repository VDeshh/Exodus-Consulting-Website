Industries / Manufacturing and distribution

The ERP holds the parts. The answers still live in inboxes.

Most shops already own an ERP. What they do not own is the hour a day spent reading orders off PDFs, answering the same three phone calls, and walking to the floor to check a number the system should already know. We build the layer that closes those gaps and leaves the ERP where it is.

How it actually runs

Before anything is built, this is the week we are describing.

No software can be scoped from a category name. These are the specifics of manufacturing and distribution that decide what is worth building and what is not.

  1. 01

    The truck leaves at three, ready or not

    Everything bends around the carrier cut-off. Picks that looked fine at ten are short at two, because a line ran long or a pallet was miscounted three weeks ago. The shipper chases the floor, the floor chases receiving, and somebody drives a box to the depot in their own car. None of it is written down afterwards, so the same shortage happens again next month.

  2. 02

    Orders arrive in six different formats

    One customer purchase order comes as a PDF attached to an email. Another comes in as an EDI 850. A third is a phone call from a buyer who has ordered the same thing for nine years and has never once used a part number. Someone reads all of them and types them into the ERP. Every typo becomes a wrong ship, a credit note and a phone call.

  3. 03

    The real stock number lives in someone's head

    The ERP says forty on hand. The floor knows eight are on hold for rework, six are in a customer's colour, and the rest are on a pallet nobody has put away. So the planner walks out and counts. That walk happens several times a day, the number that comes back is right for about an hour, and it never gets written back.

  4. 04

    Pricing and lead times are quoted from memory

    A buyer asks what a run of five hundred costs and how fast it can ship. The honest answer depends on the steel price this week, what is already on the machine, and whether the operator who runs that job is in. Most shops answer from experience, which is usually close and occasionally expensive. Nobody goes back later to check which quotes actually made money.

  5. 05

    Margin per product is a year-end surprise

    Sales, freight, scrap, rework and overtime sit in different places, so the owner sees revenue weekly and margin once a year. A product that stopped paying its way in March gets found by the accountant the following spring. By then it has been quoted at that price for twelve months and the customer has built a budget around it.

Every one of these is measurable. Most are not measured.

What we build

6 builds that pay for themselves here.

Each one states what it reads, what it does, and what a person still approves. Scope is agreed after the audit, one build at a time, against a baseline you sign.

Voice agent for order status calls

The agent answers the main line and reads a copy of the ERP: order status, promised ship date, tracking number and stock on hand by location. It closes the repeat calls, then transfers anything about pricing, a claim or a rush to the right person with a written summary already open in front of them. No order is changed on the call. A person makes every change.

Measured by: Share of inbound calls closed without a person.

Website chatbot for spec questions

It reads the product catalogue, the spec sheets, the safety data sheets, pack sizes, minimum order quantities and the published lead-time table. It answers the questions that arrive twenty times a week in writing, and passes the rest to sales with the whole thread attached. It never gives a firm price or a delivery promise. A person quotes.

Measured by: Emails landing in the sales inbox each week.

Purchase orders parsed and matched

It reads incoming customer orders as they arrive: PDF attachments, scanned faxes, plain email text and EDI 850 files. Every line is matched to a part number, a price and a customer, and scored for confidence. Clean lines queue for release. Weak matches are shown beside the original document so the reason is visible. A person releases the order into the ERP.

Measured by: Order lines typed by hand each week.

Reorder points set from real usage

It reads receipts, production output, shipments and counts per location, then sets a reorder point per part from what actually moves rather than a number somebody set years ago. It flags where the counted stock and the expected stock disagree, by part and by location, while the week is still recent. Purchasing approves every order before it goes to a supplier.

Measured by: Stockouts a month on the parts that actually sell.

One item catalogue across the systems

We list every item, price, unit of measure and tax code sitting in the ERP, the warehouse system and the accounting file, then give each field exactly one owner. Supplier part numbers, customer part numbers and internal codes are mapped into a single record. Anything unmapped goes on a list for the owner. Nothing is created or deleted silently.

Measured by: Duplicate and unmapped part numbers remaining.

A monthly pack that assembles itself

Overnight it pulls sales, freight, scrap, rework, labour hours and purchases, then builds margin by product, on time and in full by customer, and variance by location. Every figure carries the table and the date range it came from, so any number can be argued with rather than believed. The owner reviews the pack and signs it before anyone outside sees it.

Measured by: Hours spent building the month-end pack.

Where the data comes from

Your systems of record stay exactly where they are.

We read them, we do not replace them. Each one below says what we connect to, how, and the line the build does not cross.

ERP and shop floor systems

Usually: SAP Business One, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Epicor Kinetic, Genius ERP, NetSuite, SYSPRO, Odoo

What it holds. Item master, bills of material, routings, work orders, purchase orders, on-hand stock by location, standard costs and job history.

How we connect. Read through the vendor API where one exists, otherwise a nightly export into a read-only replica. Writes go back through the API on approved records only.

Where it stops. We never write to the item master, a standard cost, a bill of material or a posted transaction automatically. Those are changed by a person inside the ERP.

Warehouse, shipping and EDI

Usually: Fishbowl, Acumatica, ShipStation, Descartes, SPS Commerce, TrueCommerce

What it holds. Bin locations, lot and serial numbers, pick lists, carrier rates and tracking, and the retail document set: 850 orders, 856 shipping notices, 810 invoices.

How we connect. API for the warehouse and carrier side, SFTP file drops for the EDI side, and a webhook on shipment confirmation where the platform offers one.

Where it stops. No shipping notice and no invoice goes out to a retailer without a person releasing it. A wrong 856 is a chargeback, so that gate stays.

Quoting, CRM and accounting

Usually: Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho CRM, QuickBooks Online, Sage 50, Xero

What it holds. Customer records, quotes, price lists, credit terms, accounts receivable ageing, invoices and payment history.

How we connect. Scoped read access through the API, plus a nightly export of the receivable ageing so reporting does not hammer the live ledger during the day.

Where it stops. No price is changed, no credit limit is moved and no invoice is posted by a system. It can draft and flag. A person commits.

Built around your rules

The regimes that govern this work, and how the build answers each one.

Constraints come first, because they decide the architecture. Bring us your hosting, residency and regulatory rules at the start and we design to them rather than around them.

Regulatory and professional obligations that shape a build in manufacturing and distribution.
Regime What it demands here How the build complies
PIPEDA, with BC PIPA, Alberta PIPA and Quebec Law 25 Consent, a stated purpose and real safeguards for personal information about buyers, drivers and staff. Quebec adds breach reporting and a named person accountable for privacy. A build reads personal fields only where it needs them, access is set per role, every read is logged, and hosting stays in Canada when the client requires it.
CASL Consent, clear sender identification and a working unsubscribe on every commercial electronic message, including reorder prompts, price list updates and restock notices. Consent and where it came from sit on the customer record, the unsubscribe is honoured across every system that sends, and a suppressed contact cannot be quietly re-added by a spreadsheet import.
Safe Food for Canadians Regulations Food businesses have to trace each lot one step back and one step forward through the supply chain, and keep those traceability records for two years. Lot codes stay attached to the receipt, the production record and the shipment, so a trace runs in both directions from one query instead of three binders and a phone call.
CBSA record keeping and CUSMA certification of origin Importers keep records of origin, value and payment for six years, and hold a valid certification of origin before claiming preferential tariff treatment. Import documents are filed against the purchase order they belong to, origin certificates are stored with an expiry date, and a missing or stale certificate is flagged before the goods land.

What we will not automate

  • We do not let software send a shipping notice or an invoice to a retailer on its own. A wrong 856 becomes a chargeback and the operator wears it, so a person releases the file.
  • We do not automate a quality hold, a lot disposition or a recall decision. Those are signed by the person who carries the responsibility, and the record has to show their name.

A person stays in the loop

Anything that spends money, sends something irreversible, or carries a professional obligation arrives as a draft with a named reviewer. The system prepares the work. A person decides whether it ships.

You own the code and the data at the end of the engagement.
