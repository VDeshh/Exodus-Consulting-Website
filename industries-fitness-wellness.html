Industries / Fitness and wellness

Your billing system knows who pays, not who is leaving.

A studio or clinic runs on a booking platform, a payment processor, a charting system and a spreadsheet, and none of them agree on the same person. We build the layer that reads all four, answers the phone and the website, and puts the numbers in one place with the source named. A person still approves anything that touches money, a contract or a health record.

How it actually runs

Before anything is built, this is the week we are describing.

No software can be scoped from a category name. These are the specifics of fitness and wellness that decide what is worth building and what is not.

  1. 01

    The front desk is also the phone

    One person checks in the 6pm class, sells a drop in, takes a package payment and answers the line. At peak the phone loses. Calls go to voicemail, and a caller asking whether you offer direct billing books somewhere else instead. Nobody counts the missed calls, so the busiest hour of the week is the hour the business is least reachable.

  2. 02

    Two businesses share one reception

    A clinic with a gym floor, or a studio with treatment rooms, is two operations under one roof. The fitness side lives in a class and membership platform. The clinical side lives in a charting system with its own regulator and its own retention rules. Reception works both screens all day, and the same client exists twice with two versions of their phone number.

  3. 03

    Billing knows who pays, not who left

    The card runs on the first of the month whether or not the member has been in since March. Attendance sits in the door system, payments sit in the processor, and no report joins them. The owner finds out someone has gone when the cancellation email arrives, which is weeks after the last visit and long after anything could have been said.

  4. 04

    The schedule was set years ago

    Class times get inherited. The 9:30am was busy in 2021, so it still runs. The instructor is paid the same whether eleven people show up or three. Nobody has a clean number for revenue per class hour against the cost of running it, so a schedule change gets argued on feel and on which instructor will be upset.

  5. 05

    Insurance money arrives late and short

    Direct billing is why a lot of clients pick a clinic, and it is also where the money slows down. A claim is submitted, one insurer pays part of it, another rejects it for a missing policy number, and the remittance lands weeks later in a bank feed nobody matches back. Short paid claims are usually found by accident, if at all.

What we build

6 builds that pay for themselves here.

Each one states what it reads, what it does, and what a person still approves. Scope is agreed after the audit, one build at a time, against a baseline you sign.

Voice agent on the front desk line

It answers every call, including the ones at 6pm while the desk is checking in a class. It reads the live schedule and the client record, so it can give class times, opening hours, pricing and whether you direct bill, and it books or moves an appointment. Anything clinical, angry or contractual transfers to a person with a written summary attached.

Measured by: Calls answered on the first ring, and the share resolved without a person.

Website chatbot for the repeat questions

It reads your policies, the schedule, the price list and the insurers you bill, then answers the twenty questions that arrive every week: parking, what to bring, the cancellation window, whether a first visit needs a referral, what a package costs. It names the page it answered from. Health questions and complaints go to staff instead of being guessed at.

Measured by: Emails and messages the front desk still has to answer each week.

Intake, waiver and consent capture

The client fills one form before arriving. The system reads it, matches it to an existing record where there is one, scores that match, and drafts the entry: contact details, health history, insurer and policy number, signed waiver. Weak matches are held back rather than merged. Reception approves before anything is written into the platform of record.

Measured by: Minutes of desk time per new client, and the share who arrive with intake complete.

Membership billing against attendance

It reads the billing platform, the door scans and the class bookings, then lines them up per member. Three lists come out each morning: cards that failed, members on a paid contract who have not attended in thirty days, and people training on a lapsed one. Each message is drafted and the owner approves it before it sends.

Measured by: Dollars of failed billing recovered each month.

Insurance claim reconciliation

Every claim submitted is matched against the invoice raised and the remittance received. Short payments, rejections and claims with no answer after a set number of days land on one list with the reason beside them. Nothing is resubmitted automatically. The billing clerk reads the list and decides what goes back to which insurer.

Measured by: Dollars outstanding past thirty days, and claims written off per quarter.

Retention and win back from real attendance

Segments are built from what you already hold: first month members who came once, packages expiring with sessions unused, clients who stopped after an injury, people who only ever book the 6am. The system drafts the email or the text for each segment against the consent on file, and the owner reads and approves every send.

Measured by: Ninety day retention of new members, and reactivations per month.

Where the data comes from

Your systems of record stay exactly where they are.

We read them, we do not replace them. Each one below says what we connect to, how, and the line the build does not cross.

Member, class and membership management

Usually: Mindbody, WellnessLiving, Glofox, Xplor Mariana Tek, ABC Ignite, Perfect Gym, PushPress, Zen Planner

What it holds. Memberships and contract terms, class schedules and capacity, bookings and waitlists, signed waivers, door access records, recurring billing and package balances.

How we connect. Read-only through the platform API where one exists, otherwise a nightly scheduled export into a replica hosted in Canada. Webhooks for bookings and cancellations where the platform sends them.

Where it stops. We never cancel, freeze or alter a membership contract from outside. Contract changes are drafted for the desk and entered by a person in the platform itself.

Clinical practice management and insurance billing

Usually: Jane App, Juvonno, Noterro, ClinicSense, Practice Better, Cliniko, with TELUS Health eClaims for direct billing to insurers such as Sun Life, Manulife, Canada Life and Pacific Blue Cross

What it holds. Patient demographics, clinical charts and treatment notes, consent records, appointment history, invoices, claim submissions and remittances.

How we connect. Read-only, and only the fields the work needs: appointment times, invoice totals, claim status. Access is scoped per practitioner and every read is logged.

Where it stops. Charts are never written, summarised or copied by software. No claim is submitted, resubmitted or adjusted without the practitioner or the billing clerk approving it on screen.

Payments, retail POS and accounting

Usually: Square, Lightspeed, Stripe, Moneris, QuickBooks Online, Xero, with payroll in Payworks or Wagepoint

What it holds. Drop in sales, retail and supplement stock, card transactions and declines, deposits, unused balances on class packs, instructor and practitioner pay.

How we connect. API or scheduled export for transaction and deposit records. Card numbers never enter our systems: tokens and the last four digits only, so cardholder data stays inside the processor.

Where it stops. We do not take payments, refund a card or move money. The system flags what needs chasing and a person acts inside the payment platform.

Built around your rules

The regimes that govern this work, and how the build answers each one.

Constraints come first, because they decide the architecture. Bring us your hosting, residency and regulatory rules at the start and we design to them rather than around them.

Regulatory and professional obligations that shape a build in fitness and wellness.
Regime What it demands here How the build complies
PIPEDA, with BC PIPA, Alberta PIPA and Quebec Law 25 where they apply Consent to collect personal information, use limited to the stated purpose, safeguards proportionate to how sensitive the information is, and access on request. Quebec adds a named privacy officer, breach reporting, and disclosure when a decision about a person is made by automated means. Data stays in Canada. Each field is read for one named purpose and nothing else. Access is scoped per role, every read and write is logged, and health information is kept separate from anything used for marketing.
CASL Express or implied consent before any commercial electronic message, clear identification of the sender, and an unsubscribe mechanism that works and is honoured within ten business days. Consent and where it came from sit on the record, and a send cannot be built against a segment without it. Unsubscribes write back to every connected system the same day, so a member removed in one tool is removed everywhere.
Fitness and personal services contract rules: the Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act in BC, personal development services under Ontario's Consumer Protection Act Limits on contract length, a ten day cancellation window after the customer receives the contract, prescribed contract contents, prominent notice of cancellation rights, and set refund rules when a customer cancels on a change of circumstances. The system never cancels, renews or alters a contract itself. It flags the dates that matter, drafts the notice, and a person completes the change in the platform of record so the paperwork matches the statute.
Regulatory colleges and health information law: PHIPA in Ontario, the Health Information Act in Alberta, plus each college's record rules Registered practitioners keep records to their college's standard. Ontario massage therapy records, for example, are kept for ten years after the last visit, or ten years after a client who was a minor turns eighteen. Charts stay inside the practice management system and are never read, copied or written by our software. We take appointment and billing fields only, and retention stays under the clinic's own policy and the college rule.

What we will not automate

  • We do not let software answer health questions. Whether a sore knee needs physiotherapy or rest is a judgement a registered practitioner makes, and the system hands that call straight to one.
  • We do not cancel, freeze or renew a member's contract automatically. Consumer protection law sets the notice period and the refund, and a wrong automatic change costs you money and invites a complaint to a regulator.

A person stays in the loop

Anything that spends money, sends something irreversible, or carries a professional obligation arrives as a draft with a named reviewer. The system prepares the work. A person decides whether it ships.

You own the code and the data at the end of the engagement.
